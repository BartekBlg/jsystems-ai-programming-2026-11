# R1 / A6: Slavic languages vs English in LLM evaluations (iteration 1)

## 1. Summary
- The usual pattern holds in most primary data: English is the best or near-best language for every model family measured, and Slavic languages sit below it. Typical gaps are 2-8 points on knowledge exams and 6-18 points on commonsense/HellaSwag-style tasks.
- The only same-items comparison with a broad model set I could open is MMLU-ProX (arXiv 2503.10497, EMNLP 2025), which translates the same 11,829 questions into Czech, Russian, Ukrainian and Serbian. Averaged over 14 non-degenerate models, Czech is about 2.5 points below English (median -2.5). Strong reasoning models (Qwen3-235B-Think: CS 80.5 vs EN 80.7; o4-mini: CS 73.5 vs EN 73.7) are at near parity.
- Evidence that a Slavic language beats English exists but is sparse and weak. In MMLU-ProX, GPT-4o scores EN 59.9 but CS 70.1, SR 70.6, RU 62.0; DeepSeek-R1 scores SR 80.9 vs EN 79.5. The GPT-4o English figure looks like an extraction or formatting artifact (GPT-4.1 gets 79.8 on EN), so I do not treat these as genuine superiority.
- EuroLLM-9B (arXiv 2506.04079) and the OpenGPT-X/EU20 leaderboard paper (arXiv 2410.08928) show TruthfulQA scores above English for several Slavic languages (for example Bulgarian 56.11 vs 48.50 pre-trained). This is most likely a translation effect on TruthfulQA, not better Slavic capability. Their knowledge and commonsense tasks show English on top.
- Among non-English languages, Slavic languages are generally behind the large Western European languages on EuroLLM and OpenGPT-X tables (Czech/Polish usually at the top of the Slavic group, Ukrainian and Russian lowest on the translated HellaSwag and MMLU). The Ukrainian and Russian rows in EuroLLM are not directly comparable to English because they use ARC-Challenge in place of the ARC item set.

## 3. Details per source

**MMLU-ProX (arXiv 2503.10497).** Same 11,829 questions in 29 languages, translated with LLMs and reviewed by experts; Table 3 gives 5-shot CoT accuracy for 15 models in EN, RU, UK, SR, CS. Slavic coverage: Russian, Ukrainian, Serbian, Czech only. This is the strongest parallel-items evidence in this round. Limitations: translations were machine-produced with review; the GPT-4o EN row (59.9) is out of line with its siblings and should be re-checked; InternLM3-8B has CS 0.0 (degenerate, excluded). Appendix G has zero-shot and lite results for 36 models, not read.

**EuroLLM-9B technical report (arXiv 2506.04079v2).** Evaluates 24 EU languages with EU-translated ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU, TruthfulQA (EU20 benchmark set) plus WMT24++ (COMET-22). Appendix tables per language. Caveats: translated benchmarks are not identical items to English; Ukrainian and Russian use ARC-Challenge (Arc-C), so their ARC numbers are not comparable with the English "Arc" row. Serbian and Croatian are not covered. WMT24++ in both directions (EN→xx and xx→EN): CS 84.91/83.69, SK 83.86/83.10, SL 83.95/83.64, BG 85.65/84.06, PL 84.24/81.93, UK 84.01/82.36, RU 81.76/79.64 (post-trained). No English baseline on this task.

**OpenGPT-X / European LLM leaderboard (arXiv 2410.08928v2).** Table 1 gives per-language scores for Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct only in the fetched text. The Bulgarian average (.66) does not match the mean of its columns (about .70); verify before citing. Slavic coverage: CS, PL, SK, SL, BG; UK and RU not covered in this table; the paper says it covers 21 European languages.

**BenCzechMark (arXiv 2412.17933, TACL 2025).** 50 tasks, mostly native Czech, 14 new; AUROC-based scoring plus duel scoring. Authors state their Czech-centric models underperform multilingual models on most tasks, and that Czech models are better at language modeling than similarly sized models (attributed to Czech tokenization). No English comparison is reported in the paper; the paper says CzechBench allows "indirect comparisons" between English and Czech. Leaderboard table not extracted. A Bielik 11B v2 snippet gave a rank (21st, 49.5%); it is not from a source I opened, so it is not used.

**ZNO-Eval (arXiv 2501.06715).** Ukrainian exam benchmark with text-only and multimodal parts. The HTML URL returned 404 and the PDF was saved as binary that I could not parse (no pypdf or pdftotext in this environment). Secondary snippets quote GPT-4o results, but I did not verify them and do not report them.
