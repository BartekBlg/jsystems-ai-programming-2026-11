# A4 Report: Does language affect LLM reasoning quality? (iteration 1)

## 1. Summary
- Reasoning quality is language-dependent. English is the strongest reasoning language in nearly every primary source found, but the gap varies strongly with task, model strength and language resource level.
- Polish is NOT included in MGSM, PolyMath, BenchMAX (17 languages), or the reasoning-trace studies found. Russian and Czech/Serbian are the only Slavic languages in those sets. No primary 2025-26 study found that measures Polish reasoning accuracy against English on a matched math/logic benchmark.
- The one primary Polish-vs-English comparison found (medical exams, arXiv 2412.00559, not reasoning-focused) shows English usually higher, with the gap shrinking for the largest models (Llama-3.1-70B: +1.66 pp; gpt-4o: Polish +1.27 pp).
- Forcing the reasoning trace into the user's (non-English) language costs accuracy: about 7 points average across six reasoning models (Qwen-based distills and Skywork-OR1 on AIME/GPQA/MGSM), per arXiv 2505.22888. Left alone, reasoning traces often drift into English (only ~52% of traces match the target language on average).
- Interpretability (Schut et al. 2025; Anthropic 2025) supports an English-centric latent space, but Anthropic reports shared multilingual features (not "English thinking" as such). The strongest evidence of English centrality is in the logit-lens work on older open models.

## 3. Details per source

**Son et al., arXiv 2502.17407 (ACL 2025): Linguistic Generalizability of Test-Time Scaling in Mathematical Reasoning.** Introduces MCLM, a competition-math benchmark in 55 languages (M-IMO 38 languages, M-MO 11). Tests ORM, PRM and budget forcing on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/7B and DeepSeek-R1-1.5B derivatives, plus GPT-4o-mini and o3-mini as reference. Budget forcing yields about +20 points on English AIME but only +1.94 on average across other languages. Scaling makes cross-language consistency worse. Confounders: 1.5B-scale models, MT-generated translations, small N per language (AIME-level sets). Rebutted for larger models by Yong et al. (2505.05408).

**Qi et al., arXiv 2505.22888 (Findings EMNLP 2025): When Models Reason in Your Language.** Six reasoning models (Distill-R1 1.5B/7B/14B/32B, Skywork-OR1 7B/32B) on AIME, GPQA and MGSM, 11 languages. Without intervention, the trace matches the target language in about half of cases (MGSM is best for the 14B/32B models at 83-94%). Forcing the target language costs 4.2-10.8 points (MGSM gaps are smaller, sometimes positive for 32B). Post-training with about 100 examples partly mitigates the mismatch. Limitation: "standard" prompts use the target-language instruction, so this is not an English-forced vs target-language comparison. Polish not tested.

**Yong et al., arXiv 2505.05408: Crosslingual Reasoning through Test-Time Scaling.** s1.1 models (1.5B-32B, Qwen2.5-Instruct fine-tuned on 1k English traces) on MGSM. English-thinking gives 84.4 average at 14B; forcing reasoning into Swahili gives 67.3 and Telugu 71.5 (Table 3). Forcing target-language reasoning for low-resource languages (bn, sw, te, th) costs 7-17 points at 7B-14B (Table 2). Larger (32B) models gain from translated-wait style forcing (80.8 to 81.7 on low-resource languages). Text-table mismatch on the Swahili example (text 90.8 vs table 55.6), so cite the table. Cultural commonsense tasks: thinking more does not help.

**PolyMath, Wang et al., arXiv 2504.18428 (v4, Nov 2025).** 18 languages, 4 difficulty levels. Top-level accuracy for English vs Russian is close for the strongest reasoning models (Qwen-3-235B-Thinking 40.0 vs 38.4; Gemini-2.5-pro 39.2 vs 38.4), with bigger drops for QwQ-32B (36.8 vs 28.8) and o3-mini (25.6 vs 17.6). Some models score higher in Russian (Deepseek-R1 35.2 vs 37.6; o1-mini 20.8 vs 24.0). Polish is absent. Values are from a flattened text extraction and need checking against Table 5a in the PDF.

**Schut, Gal, Farquhar, arXiv 2502.15603.** Logit-lens analysis on French, German, Dutch and Mandarin for Gemma-2-27b, Mixtral-8x22B, Llama-3.1-70B, Aya-23-35B. Share of words routed via English: 0.70, 0.68, 0.55, 0.50. Steering with English-derived vectors works better than with target-language vectors. Evidence is about lexical concepts in the intermediate representation, not about the chain-of-thought text. Models are older (2024 generation); no reasoning models tested.

**Anthropic, "Tracing the thoughts of a large language model" (2025).** Claude 3.5 Haiku: the same circuits drive "small/opposite" prompts in English, French and Chinese; 20/27 multilingual-node features are active across all three prompts (about 74%) and 10/27 remain in pruned graphs (about 37%). The authors describe English as the default output language mechanistically, but not as the reasoning medium. Caveat: the method captures only part of the computation.

**MAPS, Hofman et al., arXiv 2505.15935 (EACL 2026 findings).** Agentic benchmark built on GAIA, SWE-bench, MATH and Agent Security Benchmark. English baseline vs 11 non-English languages. GAIA: English 47.4%, non-English mean 35.8%. ASB attack success: English 32.7%, non-English 47.3% (multilingual inputs increase vulnerability). Translation mitigations recover about 5-6 points on GAIA. Language count differs between versions (10 in v1, 11 in v2, 12 listed in latest text), so pin the version. Agents are single-backbone (GPT-o1, GPT-4.1, GPT-5-mini, Qwen-2), so these are not reasoning-trace studies. Polish not included.

**Polish-English medical (arXiv 2412.00559, EMNLP 2025 Findings).** Table 4, identical question subsets. Polish LEK vs English LEK. Most models score higher in English; the gap shrinks with scale and for GPT-4o and Bielik-11B (Polish higher). Questions are originally Polish, English are expert translations. Knowledge recall, not reasoning. This is the closest primary-source Polish-vs-English accuracy comparison found.

**BenchMAX, Huang et al., arXiv 2502.07346 (Findings EMNLP 2025).** 17 languages, 10 tasks, 6 capabilities, includes math (MGSM), tool use (Nexus), long context, instruction following. Polish not included; Russian, Serbian and Czech are. Averages across languages only in the portion read; English-vs-average in Appendix E not read. Authors state that gaps "do not invariably diminish" with model size.
