# R1 / A5 — Multilingual long-context benchmarks (Polish vs English) and tokenization premiums

Scope: Part 1 (other long-context studies that confirm or contradict OneRuler for Polish/Slavic) and Part 2 (Polish vs English token premiums in current tokenizers).

## 1. Summary

- OneRuler (arXiv 2503.01996, COLM 2025) is still the only long-context study found that includes Polish and reports Polish above English. Its §3.1 gives Polish 88% vs English 83.9% average NIAH accuracy at 64K and 128K (Polish 1st, English 6th of 26). No independent replication was found. The per-length Polish/English numbers are in a figure that could not be read as text, so only the aggregate is verified.
- The other long-context multilingual studies found (MLNeedle, mLongRR, MLRBench, MGAL) do not include Polish. Their language sets are English, German, Spanish, Hindi, Arabic, Vietnamese, Chinese (MLRBench); English, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Swahili, Somali (mLongRR); Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish (MGAL). In these, English is at or near the top, so they neither confirm nor contradict the Polish result directly.
- The only Polish-specific long-context work found (Blachnik & Chmielewski, FedCSIS 2025) is a Polish-only needle test with no English baseline. It is useful for Polish RAG but cannot test the Polish-vs-English claim.
- Tokenization is a real confounder. Modern tokenizers need about 1.8x to 2.5x more tokens per word for Polish than for English on the same content, unless the tokenizer is Polish-adapted (APT4 is about 0.8x). A fixed 128K-token window therefore holds roughly half as much Polish prose. OneRuler equalises token counts, not text, so its Polish haystacks contain less text. Neither OneRuler nor the other papers analyse this for Polish.
- Petrov et al. (NeurIPS 2023) explicitly lists "the amount of content that can be provided as context to the models" as one of the harms of tokenizer unfairness. Their appendix gives Polish premiums of 1.91 (cl100k_base) and 1.70 (LLaMA-1) relative to English, verified from the PDF.
- The citation list for OneRuler could not be retrieved: Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 429 three times, and web searches found no 2026 citing papers. This remains an open item.

## 3. Details per source

**OneRuler (arXiv:2503.01996).** Adapts RULER to 26 languages with seven tasks (five NIAH variants with varying needle count and possible absence, two aggregation tasks), at 8K, 32K, 64K and 128K. Translations were done by native speakers. The Polish result in §3.1 is an average across models at 64K and 128K, not per length. The headline gap grows from 11 to 34 points between top-5 and bottom-5 languages by Wikipedia size from 8K to 128K. Authors state that instruction language can change accuracy by up to 20%. Limitations: they compare low- and high-resource groups by Wikipedia size, which is a proxy. Tokenization is discussed in §2 (equalised token counts for context, Tamil example of 42K vs 104K tokens across tokenizers) and Appendix D. For Polish there is no token-count-per-word analysis. Confounder for interpretation: a 128K-token Polish haystack contains roughly half the words of a 128K English one with GPT-4-era tokenizers (see row 2, 3), so "Polish wins" at equal tokens means a different amount of text. The authors do not compute this.

**Petrov et al. 2023 (arXiv:2305.15425, NeurIPS 2023).** Premium = tokens for a language divided by English tokens for the same FLORES-200 sentences. Table 1 (main text) gives subset premiums for GPT-2/RoBERTa and ChatGPT/GPT-4 (cl100k) and does not include Polish. Appendix C (PDF pages 23–28) lists all 200 languages. Polish cl100k_base premium 1.91 (PDF p.27 row), Czech 2.11 (p.23), Russian 2.49, Ukrainian 3.00. Caution: the appendix has 13 columns including non-English-centric tokenizers where the premium is relative to the target language (Table 2 caption), so only the English-centric columns (LLaMA, GPT-2, cl100k_base, RoBERTa) should be compared with English. The abstract says premiums can reach 15x and that the disparity affects "the amount of content that can be provided as context to the models". These are 2023 tokenizers; newer ones (Llama 3, Gemma 3, Qwen3) are measured in row 3 and row 12 and show a similar size of gap for Polish.

**Ahia et al. 2023 (arXiv:2305.13707).** Abstract says speakers of many of the 22 languages are "overcharged while obtaining poorer results" on OpenAI's API. Polish is not named in the text, and the premium values are in figures. Text says Latin-script languages are represented with fewer tokens, and Cyrillic and Japanese "come close to the Latin script" (§4.1). Polish figure values could not be read. Ukrainian and Russian appear in figures only.

**Bielik v3 tokenizer (arXiv:2604.10799) and Bielik v3 Small (arXiv:2505.02550).** Both use one sample (Polish Constitution preamble and its English translation) and report tokens per word (TpW) and characters per token. The ratios for Polish/English: APT4 0.82x, APT3 0.77x, EuroLLM 1.48x, Gemma 3 1.83x, Mistral Small 3.1 / Apertus 1.98x, Qwen3 2.30x, Llama 3.2 / SmolLM3 2.40x, Mistral v0.1 (Bielik 11B v3 original) 2.52x. The paper says the Mistral-based tokenizer needed multiple tokens per Polish word and that the goal was to "nearly double effective Polish context capacity". It states tokenizer choice affects context length, cost and speed. Quality: the paper claims tokenization is "largely preserved", not improved; the Belebele European average drops from 82.98 to 77.41 for the PL 11B checkpoint. The authors say Polish quality depends on digit, punctuation and special-character handling. No task-quality-vs-tokenizer experiment is reported.

**The Tokenizer Tax Across 25 European Languages (arXiv:2605.24718).** Table 4 gives Polish 2.25 (min 1.88, max 2.60), English 1.23, with six locally measured tokenizers. Polish is cheaper than Ukrainian (2.66) and Czech (2.28). The paper attributes the Slavic penalty to morphology and less pre-training data (CulturaX: 117.3B tokens Polish vs 38.2B Ukrainian). Per-model Polish values are only in Figure 2 (not read). Note: the paper states "all 25 EU languages plus Ukrainian" but Ukrainian is in the 25; minor inconsistency. Status PARTIAL.

**MLNeedle (arXiv:2408.10151, NAACL 2025).** Seven languages (en, ar, de, es, hi, vi, zh), no Polish or Slavic. Finding: the needle language matters more than the haystack language; English needles score best, and non-Latin scripts (Arabic, Chinese) score worst. Authors note performance is best at the start or end of the context (lost in the middle). No token-count analysis.

**mLongRR (arXiv:2409.18006).** Five languages, Latin script, no Slavic. Table 2 reports fertility per language. Authors argue lower fragmentation correlates with better performance, which is a tokenization-as-confounder argument within their own data, though the paper does not separate it from language resource level. Per-language accuracy is shown only in heatmaps.

**MLRBench (arXiv:2504.12845).** Reasoning-oriented follow-up to NIAH, same group as MLNeedle. Table 6 shows English highest at both 4k and ≥32k. The paper's "effective context under 30%" is stated in the abstract, but its own numbers conflict (text "95%" loss vs Table 1 drop of 22.77%). It evaluates Llama-3.1-8B only and excludes API models for cost. Useful to note: none of the Polish-relevant claims can be checked here.

**MGAL (arXiv:2608.20853, 2026).** Six UN languages; Russian is the only Slavic one; Polish absent. Only Fig. 4 and App. H have per-language data; not read. The paper does not discuss tokenization in the visible text.

**Blachnik & Chmielewski (FedCSIS 2025).** Polish-only numerical NIAH in RAG over GUS statistical reports (chosen to avoid training-data contamination). Ranking from abstract: NeuralDaredevil-8B best (error rate about half of the 2nd and 3rd), then PLLuM-12B-instruct and Bielik-11B-v2.3. Note the 128K-context PLLuM was tested with inputs capped at 8192 tokens. Authors note that many open-weight LLMs lack adequate Polish support. No English baseline, so it cannot confirm or contradict Polish vs English.
