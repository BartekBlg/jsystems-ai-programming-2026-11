# R1 A3: Language effects on IR and RAG (Polish/Slavic vs English, multilingual)

Status legend: VERIFIED = numbers read in the primary source text/tables. PARTIAL = primary source opened, but numbers came from a WebFetch summarizer and were not checked against the PDF, or only some numbers confirmed. UNVERIFIED = secondary source only.

## 1. Summary

- No paper found that runs the same RAG pipeline on Polish and English corpora and reports a direct Polish-vs-English generation score. Answers to (a)-(c) are therefore indirect.
- (a) Retrieval: Polish-specific retrieval is strong in absolute terms (PIRB, PL-MTEB), but BEIR-PL shows BM25 is clearly lower on Polish than on the English original (e.g., NQ 20.1 vs 32.6, DBPedia 22.9 vs 32.1). The paper attributes this to Polish inflection/morphology. Translation of the benchmark is a confounder, so this is not a pure language effect.
- (b) Cross-lingual RAG: a 2025 study (Park & Lee, ACL Findings 2025) finds retrievers prefer query-language and high-resource-language documents, and generators in Korean/Chinese queries answer much better from same-language documents than from English documents (e.g., 49.66 vs 38.08 recall for aya-expanse-8b). Synthetic fictional-entity study (Faux Polyglot, NAACL 2025) finds the same-language bias in both retrieval and generation. This is evidence that language of documents matters, and that LLMs often prefer same-language sources, not English ones. Polish was not among the tested languages in either study.
- (c) Generation quality: no Polish-vs-English generation benchmark with matched content found. The only long-context result that puts Polish above English is OneRuler (COLM 2025): Polish is top on needle-in-a-haystack retrieval at 64K/128K (average 88%), English is 6th of 26 (83.9%). This is synthetic NIAH, not RAG QA. It is the most likely origin of the "Polish beats English" headline, but this link is UNVERIFIED (see section 4).
- Practical takeaway: language effects are real but depend on task and corpus. Results on one benchmark should not be read as a general language ranking. A matched Polish/English RAG eval (same content translated, same retriever/generator) is the only way to answer (b)/(c) for Polish.

## 3. Details per source

- **Park & Lee (ACL Findings 2025, arXiv:2502.11175).** Uses MKQA (2,827 examples per language, KILT NQ overlap subset), with Wikipedia datastores in each language and English. Retriever BGE-m3 plus MLR re-rankers; generators aya-expanse-8b, Phi-4, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, plus GPT-4o-mini in a preference analysis. Metric: MultiLingualRank (MLR) for retrieval preference and character 3-gram recall for generation. Note: the brief calls the metric "MLRS"; the paper calls it MultiLingualRank (MLR) and reports recall, not accuracy/F1. Main result: retrievers favor the query language and high-resource languages; generators favor query language and Latin-script languages. Limitations: only query languages en/ko/zh for generation; Polish not tested; MKQA is translated, so translation quality confounds results.
- **Faux Polyglot (NAACL 2025, arXiv:2407.05502).** Fictional entities so that parametric knowledge is excluded; five languages (en, hi, de, ar, zh). Strong same-language bias in retrieval (68% native-language top-10) and generation (native 42% vs foreign 29.6% answer sources). Authors list limits: only 5 languages, synthetic data, cosine retrieval only, no reranking. Polish not included. Its main lesson for Polish RAG: a Polish query tends to pull Polish sources; a multilingual corpus with mostly English sources will under-serve Polish queries.
- **XRAG (Findings EMNLP 2025, arXiv:2505.10089).** Benchmark with English and four non-English languages (de, es, zh, ar) and two setups: English-only retrieval, and English plus query-language retrieval. Accuracy is on LLM-judged QA (judge agreement with humans kappa = 0.71 per the paper). Key pattern: non-English queries score lower than English even with the same English evidence, and the gap is larger for weaker models (Nova-Pro and Mistral-large). Limitations: QA pairs generated by GPT-4o (authors say evaluation may favor GPT-4o); only 5 models; Polish and other Slavic languages not included.
- **OneRuler (COLM 2025, arXiv:2503.01996).** The English RULER benchmark adapted to 26 languages; 7 synthetic tasks (5 NIAH variants, 2 aggregation); 50 examples per language per context length (8K/32K/64K/128K). Polish top on NIAH at 64K/128K (88% avg), English 6th (83.9%). Important caveats: synthetic, not RAG QA; the ranking relies on NIAH, and the paper's aggregation tasks may rank differently (per-language results for aggregation were not extracted); the per-language table is in figures only; instruction language moves accuracy by up to 20%, so prompt language is a confounder. This is the best candidate for the source of the cryps.pl "Polish beats English" claim, but I did not verify that link.
- **PIRB (LREC-COLING 2024, arXiv:2402.13350).** 41 Polish IR tasks, NDCG@10. Multilingual-e5-large is the best single model (57.29 average); the best hybrid reaches 59.23. Only Polish is evaluated, so this is not a Polish-vs-English comparison. Some tasks were newly created by the authors (10 new datasets, including GPT-generated exam-style questions), which is a confounder for comparing to other numbers.
- **BEIR-PL (arXiv:2305.19840).** Translation of BEIR into Polish. BM25 is lower on Polish for 11 of 11 datasets vs. the English original (Table 3). Some gaps are large (NQ 32.6 -> 20.1, DBPedia 32.1 -> 22.9). The authors attribute this to morphology. Translation quality and different corpora (translated passages) both change the numbers, so the difference is not purely linguistic. Note: this comparison is BM25 only; the best Polish dense models in Table 4 are not compared to English models in the paper.
- **PL-MTEB (arXiv:2405.10138; ACL 2026 Findings version).** 30 tasks (classification, clustering, pair classification, retrieval, STS) and 30 models (Polish and multilingual). Retrieval averages show Polish-specific models (e.g., stella-pl, mmlw-retrieval) competitive with the best multilingual models, but multilingual Qwen3-Embedding-8B is best on the overall average. It is a Polish-only benchmark, so no English comparison. Numbers were read through a summarizer; re-check the PDF before citing a specific value.
- **Bartanowicz & Jassem (LREC 2026, aclanthology 2026.lrec-1.211).** Polish RAG comparison of Bielik-11B-v2.3-Instruct vs PLLuM-12B-nc-chat on low-code platform documentation. Reports Bielik consistently better in generation (pairwise A/B). The landing page has no numbers; the PDF has tables, not read. No English baseline. Related FedCSIS 2025 paper (Blachnik & Chmielewski, DOI 10.15439/2025F6165) ranks Polish-capable LLMs on RAG faithfulness: NeuralDaredevil-8B-Abliterated first, PLLuM-12B-instruct and Bielik-11B-v2.3-Instruct next. Not read; UNVERIFIED (search snippet only).

- Re-read the PDF tables for Park & Lee (Tables 1-2) and XRAG (Tables 4-5) to confirm extracted values; these are from a summarizer over HTML text.
- PL-MTEB Table 2: some values look inconsistent (mmlw-e5-base 49.80 avg below mmlw-e5-small 58.97). Verify in the PDF before citing.
- MMTEB (arXiv:2502.13595 v4), appendix per-language retrieval scores for pol, ces, rus, ukr, eng: not obtained. Next step: clone embeddings-benchmark/results and filter with the mteb package.
- No study found that compares Polish vs English answer quality in RAG with matched content. The missing experiment is: translate one RAG eval set (e.g., MKQA pl subset or PolQA) and run the same retriever and generator on both; report recall and faithfulness separately.
- Faux Polyglot and Park & Lee suggest the language of the retrieved corpus, not of the question, drives answer sources. For Polish RAG this means corpus language mix matters more than prompt language. Not verified for Polish.
- Whether OneRuler's NIAH ranking persists on aggregation tasks and on newer models (paper uses 2025 models; 2026 models not tested here).
- The FedCSIS 2025 faithfulness ranking (Blachnik & Chmielewski) needs a PDF read before use; it is a Polish-model-only comparison with no English baseline.
