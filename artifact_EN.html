Does AI really “speak best in Polish”?

The widely shared ranking in which Polish beat English comes from a single long-context benchmark (OneRuler, COLM 2025). We checked the source and gathered newer 2025–2026 evidence on knowledge, retrieval and RAG, reasoning, and agents.

ConfirmedSource: OneRuler

The article's numbers are correct: PL 88.0%, EN 83.9%. They cover only synthetic long-context retrieval.

CorrectedOver-interpretation

The paper does not study the “best language for prompting”. A co-author says it was not studied.

English ≥ PolishNewer data

In 2026 frontier models Polish is level with English; in smaller models and with tools it is lower.

Polish > EnglishThe exception

Questions about Polish realities: strong models answer better in Polish.

5 · The nuance

Questions about Poland: here Polish wins

Two 2026 papers show that for Poland-specific knowledge, prompting in Polish gives better results in strong models. On culture-agnostic questions English leads. The Masked Advantage authors explain it this way: a weaker local-language score usually reflects weaker proficiency in the language, not missing knowledge.

6 · Answers

What it means

Is Polish more effective?

Only in two narrow areas: long-context NIAH (older models, confounders) and questions about Polish realities. Elsewhere Polish is level (2026 frontier) or lower (smaller models).

Does language affect RAG?

Yes. What matters is that the query and document languages match. Models prefer sources in the question's language. Embedders lose about 1 pt in Polish; BM25 loses much more because of inflection.

Does language affect agent reasoning?

Yes, moderately. The biggest loss is in tool-argument formatting (−28 pts); math and code lose little. Reasoning forced outside English costs about 7 pts. The gap shrinks as models improve.

In practice, for GIS, Python and ETL teams

  1. Keep the corpus and the queries in the same language. For Polish documents consider Polish embedders or BM25 with lemmatization.
  2. Write tool schemas, function names and the agent's system prompt in English; the user-facing answer can be in Polish.
  3. Do not force the language the model “thinks” in.
  4. Polish text uses about 1.7–2.3× more tokens: higher cost and less content per window.
  5. Ask about Polish regulations, names and institutions in Polish.
  6. Run a small PL/EN eval on your own data instead of relying on media rankings.
7 · Method

How this report was made

17 Haiku subagent runs across 6 iterations. A lead agent managed the process, verified results, and delegated confirmation whenever something was doubtful. Only papers, preprints, official leaderboards and raw result files counted as evidence. Media served only as pointers.

  1. Evidence map. 6 agents in parallel: article source, general benchmarks, RAG, reasoning and agents, long context and tokenization, other Slavic languages.
  2. Number verification. OneRuler LaTeX sources and figures, RAG tables, Global-MMLU and model cards, MCLM, MAPS and PolyMath. pypdf was installed to read PDF tables.
  3. Citation audit. 11 arXiv IDs from 2025–2026: all exist. New data: Global PIQA, EuroEval.
  4. Replications. 39 papers citing OneRuler: no replication for Polish.
  5. Resolutions. MASSIVE-Agents table columns read from a rendered page; devil's-advocate search for counter-evidence.
  6. Local nuance. Masked Advantage and PoVisLE (2026); OneRuler haystack books verified.

Corrections made along the way

  • Bielik 82.98 → 77.41 are two different models, not a before/after result.
  • Swahili 90.8 in the Yong et al. text does not appear in the table (it is 59.6).
  • Global-MMLU does not report per-language results; the brief's premise was wrong.
  • The CUS-QA numbers from the devil's-advocate agent did not match Tab. 11, so they were excluded.
  • The lead checked in PDF and CSV: OneRuler, MCLM, MASSIVE-Agents, the Anthropic page, Global PIQA, EU-24+, Masked Advantage.
